Occupational exposure to pesticides increases the risk of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Journal: Occupational and Environmental Medicine

This meta-analysis demonstrates that occupational exposure to pesticides significantly increases the risk of developing amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), with particularly elevated risks observed for herbicide exposure and high-level contact. These findings confirm a positive correlation between pesticide exposure and ALS, highlighting a dose-response relationship where higher exposure levels correlate with greater disease risk.

מטה-אנליזה זו מדגימה כי חשיפה תעסוקתית לחומרי הדברה מעלה באופן מובהק את הסיכון ללקות בטרשת אמיוטרופית צידית (ALS), כאשר סיכון גבוה במיוחד נצפה בקרב חשיפה לקוטלי עשבים ובמקרים של חשיפה לרמות גבוהות של חומרים אלו. ממצאים אלו מאששים קשר חיובי בין חשיפה לחומרי הדברה לבין ALS, ומדגישים יחסי מנה-תגובה שבהם רמות חשיפה גבוהות יותר מתואמות עם סיכון מוגבר למחלה.

Lavigne-Robichaud et al: Job strain and ischemic heart disease: the balance of methodological bias and implications for prevention. Author reply.

Journal: Scandinavian Journal of Work Environment & Health

The authors conclude that existing epidemiological evidence is currently insufficient to either substantiate or exclude a causal relationship between job strain and ischemic heart disease. They identify a lack of independent exposure assessment and unresolved confounding by individual personality traits as primary obstacles to establishing definitive causal inference.

Return to Work & Rehabilitation

Executive Summary: Recent research underscores that subjective psychological factors—specifically personal beliefs regarding work capacity and the quality of workplace support—are critical determinants of long-term absenteeism in individuals managing health conditions. Furthermore, these findings emphasize that addressing chronic physiological or sleep-related symptoms is essential, as persistent health disturbances act as significant precursors to broader secondary clinical complications that jeopardize sustainable workforce participation. Together, these studies highlight that holistic return-to-work strategies must integrate both psychological behavioral interventions and longitudinal health monitoring to effectively mitigate the risk of prolonged disability.
